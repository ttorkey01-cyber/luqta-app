# Public reference systems: shopping search and discovery

**Research checked:** 2026-09-30
**Scope:** Public vendor documentation, vendor-published product/UI descriptions, and narrowly labeled LUQTA design inferences. This is not a reverse-engineering report. Vendor implementation details not explicitly disclosed are marked **NOT PUBLICLY SPECIFIED**; no proprietary ranking weights are assumed.

## How to read the labels

- **DOCUMENTED** — stated in official product or developer documentation.
- **OBSERVED published UI** — a vendor-published description or illustration of a customer-visible surface/behavior. This means observable in the cited publication, not independently tested in every market or account.
- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — a proposed design implication for LUQTA, not a claim about the vendor.
- **NOT PUBLICLY SPECIFIED** — the cited public material does not disclose this implementation detail.

## 2. Amazon Shopping: Rufus → Alexa for Shopping; Amazon Lens → Lens Live

**Naming distinction:** Rufus was Amazon's conversational shopping assistant introduced in 2024; Amazon announced **Alexa for Shopping** on **May 13, 2026**, bringing Rufus product expertise together with Alexa+ and Amazon shopping history/context. Amazon's own Rufus announcement now notes the rename. This is distinct from the generic, pre-existing idea of asking an Alexa voice assistant to shop: the source describes Alexa for Shopping in Amazon's shopping app/site and Echo Show, with voice/touch on Echo Show. Do not use “Alexa for Shopping” as a synonym for all Alexa voice-commerce functionality. Sources: [Rufus announcement (2024)](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus), [Alexa for Shopping announcement (2026-05-13)](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant).

Sources: [Rufus announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus), [Alexa for Shopping](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-ai-assistant). **NOT PUBLICLY SPECIFIED** — parser, intent/entity schema, model architecture, or the exact use and retention of each contextual signal. |

## LUQTA adaptation and limits

- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — keep the public patterns, not presumed vendor internals: conversational query refinement (Amazon/Perplexity), a broad product/offer corpus with structured identifiers (Google), hybrid lexical + semantic retrieval with measurable no-result recovery (Algolia), and merchant-configurable facets/boosts plus tracked buyer outcomes (Shopify).
- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — model distinct entities: canonical product, variant/SKU, merchant offer, price/availability observation, and evidence/source. Group offers only when identity evidence supports equivalence; do not merge visual similarity into product identity.
- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — use a staged system: parse explicit constraints and preferences; retrieve candidates from lexical, semantic, and (where supported) visual pathways; normalize product/offer relationships; apply hard filters; rank with transparent relevance signals; display source, price/availability timestamp, and merchant; then offer alternatives and clarifying questions when confidence/coverage is weak.
- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — treat **authorized user query images** and OCR signals as supplemental candidate generation, keep confidence and provenance, and preserve a no-match path rather than inventing a match. Do **not** send merchant, affiliate, retailer, provider-result or candidate product images to Gemini; any later candidate-visual approach requires separate rights and authorization. Support user-controlled filters and explicit feedback; evaluate changes through query-level metrics and experiments.
- **INFERRED LUQTA adaptation** — separate organic relevance from paid placement and make any sponsored result explicit. Do not silently allow commercial incentives to masquerade as relevance.
- **Limits** — vendor material is public documentation and published UI/feature descriptions as checked on 2026-09-30; feature availability can vary by country, plan, account, merchant eligibility, or time. “Observed published UI” means the vendor documented or illustrated the surface, not that this research independently tested it. Vendor claims are not independent performance evidence. Public sources do not reveal proprietary models, private weights, full data coverage, ranking implementation, all fallback behavior, or stable guarantees. Algolia NeuralSearch is plan-gated (current pricing places it under Elevate); Perplexity Instant Buy has eligibility and U.S. rollout constraints; Google product data and Amazon/Shopify catalogs are not exhaustive. LUQTA proposals above are design hypotheses, not descriptions of any vendor implementation.
